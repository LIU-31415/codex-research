# Synthesis and Research Design

Use this guidance when several studies must become an explanation, comparison, field map, or proposed study. For a brief source question, answer directly. Apply the [evidence contract](evidence-reasoning.md#minimal-evidence-and-claim-contracts) without creating duplicate records.

## Build a comparable evidence view

Organize around the question's claims, mechanisms, methods, or conditions. Choose themes from the evidence and user's decision; do not force a fixed number of themes or arrange a synthesis as disconnected paper summaries.

When a matrix makes comparison easier, use one row per relevant study, experiment, or result, retaining its publication links. A paper with several distinct conditions may need several rows; several reports of one experiment do not become several independent studies. Useful columns, selected for the task, are:

| Comparison dimension | Preserve when it affects the judgment |
|---|---|
| Question and evidence role | Which claim the result supports, contradicts, limits, or contextualizes |
| Design and independent unit | System/population, comparator, independent sample or implementation, and replication |
| Conditions and measurement | Setting, duration, scale, outcome definition, denominator, and units |
| Finding and uncertainty | Observed result or effect, uncertainty as reported, and relevant limitations |
| Provenance | Source/version, access scope, and passage, table, figure, or equation locator |

Keep unreported details, unread or inaccessible details, and genuinely inapplicable fields distinct. A blank cell is not zero, a null result, or absence of a property. Apply [fact checking](evidence-reasoning.md#fact-checking) before declaring a parameter unreported. Label derived conversions and plot estimates; retain the original values and assumptions where they matter. Do not equate repeated measurements with independent samples.

## Turn the view into a synthesis

For each material theme, explain the pattern, the evidence that bears on it, the conditions under which it holds, and what remains disputed. Use the comparison to make a judgment rather than merely restating the rows.

Compare like with like; stratify incompatible outcomes, conditions, designs, or normalization bases. A common unit alone does not make results commensurable. Describe the strongest warranted conclusion and why more direct, independent, or methodologically adequate evidence deserves greater weight. Preserve informative exceptions and use the [conflict guidance](evidence-reasoning.md#conflict-handling) when studies disagree.

Do not count statistically significant papers as votes. Non-significance alone does not establish equivalence or no effect; consider the estimate, uncertainty, design, and the size of effect the study could resolve when available. Do not invent missing intervals or pool numbers without a compatible estimand, adequate data, and an appropriate requested analysis.

For a field map, connect the themes: which methods enable which findings, which conclusions share one evidence base, and which unresolved condition limits transfer. Distinguish a lack of access, an unmeasured outcome, conflicting observations, and convincing evidence against a hypothesis. These call for different next actions.

## From a supported gap to a useful study

Apply [gap-driven retrieval](search-strategy.md#gap-driven-retrieval) first. An unexplored combination is not automatically an important question. Judge candidate directions by what uncertainty matters to the user's goal and whether a feasible observation could reduce it; novelty, value, and feasibility are separate considerations, not a universal score.

When the user wants hypotheses or a study direction, connect the proposal to:

- the specific unresolved claim and closest existing evidence;
- the plausible explanations or boundary conditions still in contention;
- the observation that would distinguish them, including a comparator or control where relevant;
- a feasible measurement or analysis and the result that would weaken the proposal;
- the assumptions, access, equipment, or data requirements that determine feasibility.

Choose only the details needed for the decision. A follow-up may be a replication, measurement check, sensitivity analysis, or transfer test rather than a new mechanism. Do not claim that a proposed discriminating test will certainly settle the issue, invent sample sizes or validated operating conditions, or silently turn a research discussion into an experimental SOP. For a requested experimental plan, follow [experimental transfer](evidence-reasoning.md#experimental-transfer).

## Keep synthesis revisable

When new evidence, a correction, or a changed scope arrives, update the affected comparison and trace which conclusions depend on it. Preserve unaffected checked claims; record whether the new material strengthens, narrows, contradicts, or leaves the conclusion unchanged. A larger paper count alone is not stronger evidence. Use the existing [research state](research-state-and-delivery.md#update-behavior) when continuity is useful and writing is authorized.
