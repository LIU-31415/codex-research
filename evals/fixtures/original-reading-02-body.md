# SYN-READ-02 — Original article body stand-in

## Introduction 1 — page 1

Sensor drift can limit indoor monitoring. This pilot examines recalibration under a stable environment; broader deployment is a question for future validation.

## Methods 2.1 — page 2

Eight independently assembled modules were evaluated in two occupied rooms. Each module produced 4,800 repeated observations. A linear correction was fitted using an initial calibration interval; the later evaluation used the same rooms. The outcome was relative reduction in mean absolute error against a reference instrument, not an accuracy percentage. Inference used module-level resampling. Deployment duration, temperature conditions, and low-level-data handling are in [Supplement S1](original-reading-02-supplement.md).

## Discussion 4.1 — page 4

The estimate is compatible with benefit in this pilot, but its interval includes no reduction. The study did not test independent buildings, a temperature range, or long-term service. Temperature was held stable; the design did not separate its effect from sensor age and does not identify a mechanism.

## Conclusion — page 4

The pilot gives a bounded short-term estimate with uncertainty. Further independent deployment and controlled comparisons are needed before claiming reliable broader benefits.
