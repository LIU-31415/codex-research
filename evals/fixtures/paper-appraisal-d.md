## Introduction — page 1

The study asks whether recalibration reduces MAE compared with leaving devices unchanged under matched field conditions. It does not assess performance in every environment or long-term durability.

## Methods §2 — page 2

Twelve independent devices were evaluated at two field sites against the same type of validated reference instrument. Sampling frequency and measurement protocol were held constant in both groups. Devices were assigned to recalibration or no recalibration; [Supplement S1](paper-appraisal-supplement.md) describes allocation, independent units, uncertainty estimation and follow-up. The outcome is MAE in measurement units; the reported effect is a between-group difference in changes from baseline.

## Results §3 — page 3

Difference in changes (recalibration minus control): −1.0 unit; 95% interval −1.5 to −0.5 units.

Footnote: a negative difference represents greater MAE reduction in the recalibration group. The interval is from the device-level analysis described in S1. This is not an accuracy percentage, an interval across all possible sites, or a 90-day estimate.

## Discussion §4 — page 4

The controlled comparison supports a benefit in the evaluated deployment, subject to the small number of independent devices and sites. No other seasons, device types or longer duration were evaluated. Raw data and code are not supplied, so external reproduction cannot be claimed from this article.

## Conclusion — page 4

Recalibration reduced MAE relative to the concurrent control over the tested ten-day deployment at two sites. More settings and longer follow-up remain to be evaluated. This scope agrees with the abstract.
