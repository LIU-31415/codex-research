## Introduction — page 1

Sensor drift can limit indoor monitoring. The study asks whether recalibrating an existing batch reduces measurement error against a reference instrument. The motivation includes longer-term deployment, but motivation alone is not a tested outcome.

## Methods 2.1 — page 2

Eight independently assembled modules were tested in two occupied indoor rooms. Each module yielded 4,800 repeated observations. A linear correction was fitted during an initial calibration session and evaluated during a later session in those same rooms. The target metric is relative reduction in mean absolute error against the reference instrument; it is not percentage accuracy. The analysis resamples modules, not individual readings.

## Discussion 4.1 — page 4

Recalibration is compatible with a benefit in the tested setup, but the interval in Table 2 does not establish a reliably positive reduction. Temperature stabilization is a possible explanation, not an identified mechanism: there was no intervention separating temperature from sensor age. Training and evaluation occurred in the same rooms, and no new-building test was performed. The title and abstract describe the intended contribution more broadly than the observations justify.

## Conclusion — page 4

The pilot motivates controlled testing of environmental conditions and independent deployment validation. General efficacy across buildings, temperatures, and longer service durations remains unestablished.
